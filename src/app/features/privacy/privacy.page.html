Who processes your data

FitSync is a fitness and diet app built and run by the FitSync team as a software engineering capstone project. The team decides what data the app collects and how it is used. Amazon Web Services (AWS) hosts the app and processes the data on the team's behalf, and it also provides sign-in (Amazon Cognito), email delivery (Amazon SES) and the AI model behind the coach (Amazon Bedrock).

What we collect and why

Account
Your email address, your role and a record of each device you are signed in on. We use them to sign you in, to keep your account secure and to let you see and sign out your devices in Settings. Amazon Cognito holds your password; FitSync never sees it.
Profile
Your display name, birth date, sex, height, activity level, time zone and, if you add one, a profile photo. Your age, sex, height and weight are used to estimate how much energy you need each day.
Health readiness answers
Your yes or no answers to six screening questions, such as whether you have a heart condition, any injury areas you choose and the time you acknowledged that FitSync is not medical advice. We use them to keep your training safe. Exercises that load an injured area are left out of the plans the coach drafts.
Body metrics and goals
Your weight, body fat and body measurements over time, and the targets you set. They drive your daily calorie and macro targets and the charts on the Progress pages.
Meals
The meals, foods, recipes and water you log. We use them to total your day against your targets and to show trends over time.
Training
Your workout plans, sessions, sets and records. We use them to plan your week, to track progress and personal records, and to award achievements.
Coach conversations
The messages you send the coach, its replies, the page readings it writes and the plan drafts it makes. They are stored so the coach can answer in context and you can see what it said.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for advertising.

Health data and your consent

Your readiness answers and your body data are health data. FitSync asks for your explicit consent before it saves them, during setup, and records your answer on your account. Setup cannot be finished without that consent, because your targets and plans are worked out from this data.

You can withdraw your consent at any time in Settings, under Privacy and data. Withdrawing consent stops the coach from using your health answers: it no longer leaves out exercises for the injury areas you chose. Your logs stay, and deleting your account erases everything, as described below.

Where your data is stored

Your data is stored by AWS in its Asia Pacific (Singapore) region. The database and the media files are encrypted at rest, and all traffic between your browser and FitSync uses HTTPS. The app's pages are delivered through Amazon CloudFront, which serves them from locations close to you around the world. The one exception to processing in Singapore is the AI coach, described in the next section.

The AI coach

When the coach writes a reply, a page reading or a plan draft, FitSync builds a prompt from your question and from facts it has worked out from your own data, such as your weight, the meals you logged, how many sessions you trained this week or how your intake compares with your targets. That prompt is sent to Amazon Bedrock, which runs the AI model.

The questions you ask the coach and the data it reads are processed by Amazon Bedrock and may be processed outside Singapore. FitSync uses Bedrock's global cross-region inference, which can send a prompt to an AWS region in another country to answer it.

Your health readiness answers are never included in a prompt. While you consent to health data, the coach uses the injury areas you chose to leave matching exercises out of its plan drafts. After you withdraw consent it no longer receives them.

Emails

Email notifications are opt-in. They are off for every new account, and each kind of email has its own switch in Settings, under Notifications. Every email says why you received it. The one email sent whatever your settings are is the notice that confirms a request to delete your account, because it is a security notice. Emails are delivered by Amazon SES in Singapore.

How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as you have an account. When you ask to delete your account, FitSync signs you out on every device and refuses new sign-ins. You have 30 days to change your mind by signing in again. After those 30 days your account and its data are erased.

Database backups are kept for 7 days, so erased data can remain in a backup for up to 7 more days before it is gone for good. When you delete a single meal, recipe or workout, it is hidden from you and erased together with your account.

Your rights

  • Access and export. You can request an archive of your data in Settings, under Privacy and data, and download it as one JSON file. The file holds your account, profile, preferences, goals, body metric history and readiness answers, your meals, foods and recipes, your workout plans and sessions, and your coach conversations and the drafts and readings the coach wrote.
  • Correction. You can edit your profile, preferences, readiness answers, goals and body entries at any time in the app.
  • Deletion. You can delete your account in Settings, under Privacy and data. You can undo the request by signing in within 30 days.
  • Withdrawing consent. You can withdraw your consent to health data in Settings, and turn off any email at any time.

If you have a question or a request these controls do not cover, write to us at the address below.

Other services the app uses

None of the services below receives your account, health or activity data.

  • Food data comes in part from Open Food Facts, available under the Open Database License (ODbL), and from the USDA FoodData Central dataset. This data is copied into FitSync, so looking up a food does not contact either service.
  • Recipe photos are loaded from TheMealDB, so your browser connects to themealdb.com and shares your IP address with it when a photo is shown.
  • Food brand logos are loaded from Google's favicon service. When a logo is shown, your browser sends your IP address and the brand's web address to Google.
  • The app's fonts come from Google Fonts, and some exercises play a video from YouTube, in its privacy-enhanced mode, or from Vimeo. Your browser contacts these services when it loads them.
